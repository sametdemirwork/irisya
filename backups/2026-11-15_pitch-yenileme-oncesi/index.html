FAQs

Clear answers, before you choose a colour

Straight answers about keratopigmentation, laser lightening, safety, prices and travel to Istanbul, from the team that plans your new shade.

How much does eye colour change cost in Istanbul?

Your price depends on the method, whether you treat one or both eyes and how many nights you stay. Send us daylight photos of your eyes for a fixed, all-inclusive quote, with the procedure, 5-star hotel and VIP transfers included.

What's included in the all-inclusive package?

Your online assessment and shade preview, a full eye examination with corneal mapping, the procedure, aftercare drops and sunglasses, your 5-star hotel stay, VIP transfers between airport, hotel and clinic, a personal coordinator and remote follow-ups.

Is the price fixed, or can it change when I arrive?

Your quote is fixed once our team has reviewed your photos and medical history. If your eye exam in Istanbul shows you're not a suitable candidate, we won't treat you, and we'll explain before you book how your deposit is handled in that case.

How do I pay, and is a deposit required?

A small deposit secures your dates and hotel. The balance is paid at the clinic by card, bank transfer or cash, and you receive a full itemised invoice.

Is the online assessment really free?

Yes. Send a few daylight photos of your eyes and answer a short medical questionnaire. We reply with a shade preview, a first view on candidacy and a fixed quote, with no obligation to book.

What is laser keratopigmentation?

A technique that changes how the colour of your eyes looks without touching the iris. A femtosecond laser creates a fine ring-shaped tunnel in the cornea, the clear front window of the eye, and a micronised mineral pigment in your chosen shade is placed inside it. The centre is left clear, so you see through your pupil as before.

How is it different from laser iris lightening?

Keratopigmentation adds a chosen colour over your iris, so you can pick an exact shade, including lighter ones. Laser iris lightening removes some of your own brown pigment, so lighter tones show through gradually over weeks, and the final shade can't be chosen precisely. Your ophthalmologist will explain which suits your eyes.

Do you offer iris implants?

No. Leading ophthalmology organisations have warned against cosmetic iris implants because of serious risks such as raised eye pressure, cataract and corneal damage, so we don't offer them.

Is the procedure painful, and how long does it take?

Numbing eye drops are used, so most people feel only light pressure. Keratopigmentation takes about 20 minutes for both eyes in most cases, and you go back to your hotel the same day. Light sensitivity, watering and a gritty feeling are common for a few days afterwards.

Which colours can I choose?

Eight signature shades, from Glacier Blue to Honey, or a custom blend. Because the pigment is layered over your iris, dark eyes can go lighter. We'll guide you toward shades that look natural with your skin tone and hair colour.

When will I see my new colour?

With keratopigmentation, straight away. The tone can look slightly more intense at first and settles over the following weeks. Laser lightening is different: changes appear gradually over several weeks.

Is it permanent? Can it be reversed?

Keratopigmentation is designed to be long-lasting. A shade can be deepened or adjusted, and pigment can be partly removed, but complete reversal can't be guaranteed, so treat it as a permanent decision. Laser lightening cannot be reversed.

Am I a good candidate?

Generally adults with healthy eyes and enough corneal thickness. A full exam decides. You may not be a candidate if you have conditions such as keratoconus, uncontrolled glaucoma, active eye inflammation or severe dry eye, or if you're pregnant or breastfeeding.

What are the risks?

Like any eye procedure, it carries risks. The most common are temporary light sensitivity, glare and dry eye. Less common are infection, inflammation, uneven or fading pigment that needs a touch-up, and visual disturbances such as halos in low light. Laser lightening can also raise eye pressure.

Your ophthalmologist goes through every risk with you, in writing, before you decide.

Will it affect my vision?

The central zone of the cornea is left clear, so your line of sight is not covered. Some people notice glare or light sensitivity, especially in dim light when the pupil widens. Your ophthalmologist explains how this applies to your eyes before you decide.

I've had LASIK. Can I still have it?

Often, yes, but it depends on your corneal thickness and shape after laser surgery. Send us your records and we'll assess them before you travel.

Can I wear contact lenses?

Usually, once your eyes have healed and your ophthalmologist agrees. Lenses can change corneal measurements, so we'll tell you how long to leave them out before your exam.

Will future eye doctors need to know?

Yes. Tell any eye doctor about your treatment. You'll receive a treatment passport with your shade code, technique and dates, which helps with future eye exams and procedures such as cataract surgery.

How long do I need to stay in Istanbul?

Most patients stay 3–5 days: an eye exam on arrival, the procedure the next day and check-ups before you fly home. Your ophthalmologist confirms when you're cleared to fly.

Which airport should I fly into?

Either Istanbul Airport (IST) or Sabiha Gökçen (SAW). Send us your flight details and your private driver will be waiting at arrivals with your name.

Which languages do your coordinators speak?

English, Arabic, German and Romanian, and we can arrange interpreters for other languages. Your coordinator stays with you from your first message to your last follow-up.

Can a family member or friend come with me?

Of course. Your companion can share your hotel room and all VIP transfers. Just tell your coordinator when you book.

Do I need a visa to travel to Türkiye?

Many visitors can enter Türkiye visa-free for short stays; others can apply online for an e-Visa. Rules change, so please check the official requirements for your passport before you book flights.

What should I avoid after the procedure?

For about two weeks: rubbing your eyes, swimming pools and the sea, saunas, eye make-up and dusty places. Wear sunglasses outdoors and use your drops exactly as prescribed. Your written aftercare plan gives the details for your treatment.

What if I have a problem after I fly home?

Message your coordinator on WhatsApp with photos. Our ophthalmologists review them, advise you remotely and, if needed, help you see an eye doctor locally or arrange a visit to Istanbul.

Will I need follow-up checks?

Yes. You'll have check-ups before you fly, then remote follow-ups at agreed intervals. A local eye doctor can also examine you using your treatment passport.

When can I go back to work?

Many people return to desk work within a few days, once light sensitivity eases. Your ophthalmologist will advise you based on your job and how your eyes recover.

Will people be able to tell?

At normal conversation distance, most people simply see the colour. Up close, an eye care professional can tell. Choosing a natural-looking shade makes the biggest difference.

Still have a question?

Message our patient coordinators on WhatsApp, in English, Arabic, German or Romanian. Most replies arrive within minutes.
